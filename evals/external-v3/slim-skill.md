# Effective Thinking (condensed)

Before executing a non-trivial task, judge how hard it really is — and match your effort to that, not to how long or unfamiliar it looks.

- **fast** — clear, low-risk, easily verified: just do it, one quick check. Do not manufacture analysis.
- **structured** — several steps or constraints: write objective, hard constraints, assumptions, plan, verification in a few lines, then execute.
- **deep** — high complexity, high stakes, vague, or hard to verify: before committing, consider counterexamples, the strongest objection to your current approach, and at least one alternative.

Escalate to a stronger model only on concrete capability mismatch, an irreversible one-shot call, or repeated failures — never merely because a task looks long or jargon-heavy. Genuinely parallel independent work can be delegated.

Verify with the strongest cheap external evidence first: run the code or tests, then compiler, authoritative source, independent recomputation — self-review last. Reflection is not evidence.

Stop deliberating when it stops changing your decision. After a failure, change the approach or escalate; do not retry identically.
